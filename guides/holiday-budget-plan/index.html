How to make a holiday budget: a 5-step plan

Holiday overspending usually is not one big purchase. It is the gifts plus everything around them: the dinner, the train tickets, the wrapping paper, the extra round of cards. This plan puts all of it on one page before you shop.

The 5 steps

  1. Set the limit first. The most you can spend without carrying a balance into January. Everything else has to fit inside this number.
  2. List gift groups, not just names. Family, kids, friends, work and teachers, partner. People × amount per person for each group makes the gift total obvious.
  3. Add the costs people forget. Food and hosting, holiday travel, decorations, cards and postage, wrapping, events and outings, donations, an outfit for a party.
  4. Compare with the limit. If the plan is over, trim the largest lines first: a lower amount for one big group, or a gift swap instead of individual gifts, moves the total more than cutting one present.
  5. Turn it into a weekly number. Plan total minus what you have already set aside, divided by the weeks left.

Worked example

Gifts: family 6 × 60 = 360, kids 4 × 40 = 160, friends 5 × 25 = 125, work and teachers 3 × 15 = 45, partner 120 → 810
Everything else: food and hosting 250, travel 150, decorations 60, events 50, donations 40, cards and postage 30, wrapping 20 → 600
Plan total 1,410, limit 1,500 → 90 under
Already set aside 300, 54 days (7.7 weeks) left → (1,410 − 300) ÷ 7.7 = about 144 a week

In this example gifts are 57% of the season. That is common, and it is why the "everything else" list matters: 600 of spending would have been invisible on a gifts-only list.

Checklist of easy-to-miss costs

  • Hosting groceries and drinks
  • Gas, train or flights to see family
  • Gift wrap, tape, tags, bags
  • Cards and stamps, shipping for gifts sent by mail
  • Teacher, coach and neighbor gifts
  • Office gift swap
  • Holiday markets, shows and outings
  • Party outfit
  • Donations and toy drives
  • Tree and decorations

Debt snowball vs avalanche: a worked example with real numbers

Most explanations stop at "smallest balance first" versus "highest rate first". Here are both methods run month by month on the same five debts, so you can see what actually changes: the order, the timing and the interest.

The avalanche saves 233.36 and finishes one month sooner. The snowball pays off its first debt (the medical bill) in month 2; the avalanche's first win (the store card) comes in month 3.

Watch the store card column: once the medical bill is gone, its 50 minimum plus the extra lands on the store card. That growing payment is the "snowball".

Which one should you pick?

If the interest gap is small, as here, the snowball's quick wins can be worth the extra cost because they keep you going. If you have one large, high-rate balance, the avalanche gap gets much bigger and is usually worth it. The only way to know is to run your own numbers.

For comparison, with no extra money at all (minimums only, with paid-off minimums still rolling over), the same debts take 42 months and 4,703.83 in interest. The 300 extra cuts about 17 months and roughly 2,300 in interest.
